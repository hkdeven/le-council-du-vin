# Le Council du Vin · what has changed

A running record for the members: every release, and the unseen work behind it. Newest first.

## 19 October 2026, later

A promise kept in earnest.

- **Your wine is now secret from the vault as well as from the page.** The Council has always told you that the wine you log is hidden from every other soul, even the Keiser, until the reveal. The app honoured that; the database did not. Its rule said any signed-in account could read every wine logged for the coming night, with its price and whose it was. Nobody had to be clever: it was one request from a browser, and nothing recorded that it happened. There is no sign anyone ever did, and the rule is now what the promise always said: you read your own wine whenever you like, and anyone's only once the cloths have lifted, which means once every soul who answered the call has sealed their ballot. The Keiser is not excepted, which is the whole point.
- **Sealing a second wine now asks first.** You could always come back and change your offering, but the second sealing happened in silence, and a scan can rewrite all three answers in one tap. Now it names what it is about to replace and waits for you to say so. Correcting a price or a grape goes straight through.
- **The vine moved indoors.** The Prophecy has to weigh every wine in the room before it can name a winner, and it used to do that on the Keiser's own phone, which is precisely why the vault had to be left open. It now reads the room on the Council's server and hands back one name. Nobody's bottle passes through anybody's device, the Keiser's included.

Behind the curtain: the new rule was proved on a throwaway copy of the real database, impersonating a genuine member rather than the Keiser, whose account passes most tests whether or not they work. Fifteen checks: a member sees their own wine and no other, cannot find another's by name, cannot sweep the table, the Keiser is refused the coming night, a stranger with an account reads nothing, a night opens the moment the last ballot is sealed, a sleeping seat is never waited on, and a committed night stays open for ever. The same fifteen were then run against the old rule, which failed eight of them, so the checks are known to bite rather than merely to pass.

## 19 October 2026

Warnings before you pour, and the Council's hour settled.

- **You are told when somebody has already logged your wine.** Name a bottle that another soul has logged for the same night and the page stops you with a notice: bring it if you must, but the two will be pooled and averaged into one standing, so you are encouraged to bring something else. It never says who the other soul is.
- **And when the Council has drunk it before.** A wine already in the annals brings up a second notice under its name, saying which gathering met it, who carried it and what it scored, and pointing out when your vintage differs from the one tasted. Neither warning stops you sealing anything.
- **Scan the label from the app too.** The camera was only on the page your summons opens; the offering form on Convene now has it as well.
- **The Council pours at 18:30.** Every new gathering is set to half past six unless its night says otherwise, and the hour the calendar invite carries, the hour the rite opens by, and the hour the summons prints all follow the same single figure.
- **The crowned wine opens.** It was the one bottle on the Reckoning whose verdicts could not be read, because it is drawn apart from the rankings. It now opens like any other.
- **Several wines at once.** Opening one wine's verdicts no longer closes the last.
- **Spoiled and cast out now read beside the bearer's name** rather than falling onto a line of their own.
- **The letter reads better.** Its second button, Meeting details, is now an outline so it stops competing with the one above it, and that one now says Log your wine.

Behind the curtain: the Council's hour had been written out by hand in seven places and could have drifted apart; it is one constant now, and the suite that guards the calendar invite caught the change, which also revealed that those 39 checks had never been part of the standard run. They are. Matching one wine against another is guesswork, so 51 checks hold the rules to account, most of them about what must NOT match: two wines from one estate are not one wine, a bare estate name sweeps up nothing, and a single shared word never joins two bottles.

## 18 October 2026, later

The Reckoning given some ceremony, and the camera put to work.

- **Scan the label instead of typing.** On the page your summons opens, there is now a camera. Photograph the bottle, or pick a photograph you have already taken, and the three answers fill themselves in: the wine and its vintage from the label, the grape from the label or from what the wine is known to be, and a price. Every line stays yours to correct, and nothing is sealed until you say so. One honest note about the money: the figure is what the wine typically sells for, not what you paid, so if you caught it on sale or it was a gift, change it. If the photograph is too dark or too partial to read, the page says so rather than inventing a bottle for you. The photograph is sent to be read and kept nowhere, and it is shrunk on your own phone first, so it works on a weak signal at the table.
- **Why your wine is asked for before the night.** The page now says plainly what the Prophecy is, why it cannot speak until every soul who answered has named a bottle, and that nothing you write there is seen by anybody, the Keiser included, until every ranking has been revealed.
- **The Reckoning unfolds.** Open it and the crowned wine arrives first, then each standing in turn, then the night's closing passages, rather than the whole page landing at once. If your phone is set to prefer less motion, you get it all immediately.
- **The bolt now says what it is.** A lightning mark used to sit alone on a line beside a wine, meaning nothing to anyone. It now rides beside the name and reads "split the table", which is what it always meant: the bottle the table most disagreed about.
- **Smaller things.** The split cloth's finding sits on its own line. "Add photo of bottles" and "Email this to me" are the same button now, with the three moons parting them from the record above.
- **The Keiser can amend the night after it is sealed.** Binding two souls' identical bottles, and marking one spoiled, were only possible before committing; now they sit on the Reckoning itself.

Behind the curtain: a fault found before it could bite. The record had been stamping a bound wine's average and its combined vote count onto each of its bottles, so the Keiser amending a sealed night would have averaged an average and doubled the votes on every save, and loosening a binding could never have given each glass back what the table actually gave it. Each bottle now keeps its own verdict and takes only the group's standing. 42 further checks guard the reading of a label, including that a blurred photograph comes back unsure rather than confident, that a price range is read at its lower end, and that no invented bottle can enter the record.

## 18 October 2026

The first gathering that went without a fault, and the table's own requests, built.

- **The pour number can no longer be mistaken for the place.** Until now the number on a bottle and the place it took were both roman numerals in the same lettering, so a line read "II, Bottle III" and nobody could tell the order it was poured from the standing it earned. The pour number is now a plain number inside a circle, and it always trails the name. The place stays roman. The two can never be read as the same thing again.
- **A claimed wine keeps its number.** Naming a bottle used to erase its number along with the word "Bottle", so the moment a wine was claimed the night lost all record of when it was poured. Now only the word gives way: "Bottle 5" becomes "Porseleinberg 2021 5", with the circled number still beside it.
- **The victor wears a face.** The soul who brought the winning bottle now appears beside it on the Reckoning, and nobody else does.
- **Every bottle opens to the whole table's verdicts.** Tap any wine on the Reckoning and it unfolds to show what each of us gave it and what each of us wrote, your own verdict pinned to the top. The score bars under each wine are gone, and the heading says so plainly: tap a wine for every verdict.
- **The table's whispers are gone.** Three anonymous notes used to float at the foot of the night, detached from the wines they were about. Every note now lives on the bottle it was written for, where it means something.
- **Two souls, one wine.** When the same bottle is brought twice, the Keiser can now bind the cloths together: they become a single entry carrying every pour number, the average of every verdict cast on any of them, and one shared standing, exactly as two wines tying for a place already share one. Each bearer still brought a bottle, so each is still credited with it, and if the wine takes the night all of them are crowned. The binding can be loosened again at any time.
- **A spoiled bottle counts against nobody.** A bottle that comes up bad measures the bottle, not the soul who carried it. The Keiser can now mark one spoiled, on the night or later in the codex. It stays in the record for ever, keeps its pour number, and takes no standing, no score, and no weight in anyone's average, their coin, their purse or their palate. It is counted as a bottle brought, because it was. It is deliberately not the same as a wine cast out: being cast out counts against a soul, and enough of it summons them before the tribunal, which no spoiled cork should ever do.
- **Name your bottle from the letter itself.** Your summons now carries a button. One tap opens a page with three questions, the grape, the name of the wine and what it cost, and no password to remember. Seal it and there is nothing to type on the night. You may change it until the cloths lift, and it stays hidden from every eye, the Keiser's included, exactly as it does in the app. Anyone who would rather use the app still can. The Keiser can also send a single nudge to whoever has not named a bottle yet.
- **The number of wines now reaches the table.** When two souls did not arrive and the Keiser cut the count mid-night, every phone that already had the rite open kept waiting for verdicts on bottles that were never poured, and only a reload freed them. The rite now notices a change within seconds, says so in one line, and lets the seal through.
- **The Reckoning stays where you left it.** A reveal is promised for a week, but it was being shown for the night the Council was working towards, so convening the next gathering made the last one's Reckoning unreachable long before its week was up. The reveal now holds on the night just held until its week truly runs out.
- **Smaller words.** "Look upon the bottles" is now "Add photo of bottles". "As the table ranked them" is now "Rankings". The champion's mark reads "Champion".

Behind the curtain: the arithmetic of a night, binding and spoiling included, was lifted out into one place of its own and put under 63 checks, among them three identical bottles one of which was corked, a bound pair where only one cloth was cast out, juries of different sizes (the average is of the verdicts, never of the averages), and a night where every bottle was spoiled. Another 23 checks guard the writing of the pour number and 16 guard which night the reveal shows and for how long, including the exact fault reported above. The Council's suites now stand at 341 checks, all green. The link in the summons is a key cut for one soul and one night: it can write that soul's offering and nothing else, it is refused once the cloths have lifted, it is refused for a sleeping seat, and it is kept in a table of the vault that no member's key can read at all.

## 24 September 2026

One repair, for the wound that would not close, and one new room in the Heavens.

- **The Atlas.** Open your Wheel and it now offers two views: **The sky**, the round chart you know, and **The earth**, the same chart laid flat on a map of the world. At the minute you were born each planet was rising somewhere, overhead somewhere, setting somewhere and directly below somewhere; joined up, those places make four lines per planet across the globe, and near a line that planet's themes are said to come forward. The map shows five planets at first; tap a symbol to see one planet alone with its nearest cities marked. Under it, the cities the sky favours you in, sorted by what you might be looking for (love, luck and money, work, home, and the hard lessons too, because a reading that only flatters is not a reading), each saying how far the line passes and how strongly. **Select a place**: name any town and read, in plain words, what your chart says about you being there. And for full members and the Keiser only, **the council's map**: everyone's lines for one theme on one map, and the cities where the most of us have a line nearby, which is to say where the council would travel well together; and **kindred ground**, the places where your welcome lines and another member's meet, so you know who to travel with and where. Each city is named exactly, with its nearest big city and its country beside it in a quieter voice, and a sixth question, **fun**, sits between love and luck. Every word in this room is plain English on purpose. Its envelope sends you the map and the city lists. Behind the curtain: the lines come from the very same planet positions as your Wheel, no outside service is consulted, the world's coastlines are drawn by the Council itself, and forty-one checks guard the geometry, including that the Sun's underfoot line falls where a two-in-the-morning birth demands. Nothing is asked of you: if your Wheel is drawn, your Atlas is drawn. Later the same day the Keiser doubted the lines, rightly: two flaws were found and closed. The planets had been placed on the ecliptic itself, when the Moon wanders five degrees from it and Pluto seventeen, and Pluto's position model was two degrees out. Every planet's latitude is now computed, Pluto follows the Meeus series (on your Wheel too), and all ten planets are checked against the Swiss Ephemeris, the reference the professional astrologers' software uses, for ten birth records across both hemispheres and seven decades: the worst disagreement is six hundredths of a degree, about seven kilometres on the ground. A hundred and twenty checks now guard the Atlas, a third of them about what happens when a new member joins: an initiate never appears on the council's map, a sleeping seat never does, and a record missing its hour or its town is veiled rather than guessed. Also: the eleven birth rows, Element through Day sign, have left the member card and now open your Wheel in the Heavens, and on your profile The Heavens door sits right under your portrait.

- **Your claim now reaches the table.** On two gatherings running, every soul but the Keiser found their bottle unclaimed when the night was committed, and the Keiser had to name each hand by hand in the codex afterwards. It was not a refusal this time. When you tapped "claim this bottle" on the revelation, your name was written into your own phone and nowhere else: nobody at the table saw it, and the Keiser's commit, made from the Keiser's phone, never knew of it. A claim is now written to the vault the moment you tap, on your own offering, and every phone at the table, the Keiser's above all, shows it within a few seconds. The wine, grapes and price you logged before the night ride along with it, as they always meant to. One hand per bottle is a rule of the vault now: if two souls tap the same bottle in the same breath, the second is told it is already claimed, not quietly dropped. Behind the curtain: a new column on the offerings table, twelve checks run as a real non-Keiser member against a local copy of the live vault, and twenty checks on the laying of claims over the tallies, each suite shown going red first. Nothing is asked of you before the night; the column fills only from the revelation. A second, independent pass over the repair then closed six smaller gaps the same day: if the vault cannot be reached at the very moment the Keiser commits, nothing is written and the Keiser is told, rather than the record being sealed with every hand blank; a page left open through the commit now turns into the Reckoning by itself; the Keiser sees every name on the Reckoning the instant it is sealed; no hand but yours can move, take or erase your claim; erasing your wine no longer erases your claim with it; and a slow phone can no longer briefly show your claim as not taken.

## 11 September 2026

A hard testing pass before the coming gathering. Most of what follows is repair, and one of them is the same wound as last time, found still open.

- **The sealed reckoning is final in earnest now.** The mend made in August closed the door only once the page had finished opening. The orb filled, and the verdict went nowhere. That window is closed, and a verdict that genuinely cannot be written now says so beneath your ballot instead of failing in silence.
- **Claiming your bottle no longer races the table.** Every soul claims in the same half-minute after the cloths lift, and all the claims are written into a single record. Two landing together meant the second quietly erased the first, with both members told they had succeeded. Claims are now taken one at a time, so every name holds.
- **Your offering cannot fail in silence.** If sealing an offering was refused, the screen said it was sealed and the Prophecy went on insisting that offerings were still outstanding. A refusal is now shown to you.

Your record could be readable by you and still not be found for you, which is exactly the fault that shut a member out for six weeks. Both halves now agree, and no soul can be turned away by the spelling of their own address.
- **The Keiser may erase a night again.** Striking a gathering from the annals had been refused outright since the record was sealed, and the refusal was reported as success, so the night vanished from the convening and stayed in the codex for ever.

## 11 July 2026

The Council's instrument has been rebuilt in near silence, and the work is now yours. One list, everything new:
